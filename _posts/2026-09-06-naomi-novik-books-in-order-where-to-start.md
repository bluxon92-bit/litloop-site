Temeraire is Napoleon's wars fought with dragons in the sky, and it is one of the best ideas fantasy has produced this century. I picked up _His Majesty's Dragon_ in my early teens with zero expectations beyond "dragons, cool," and came out the other side genuinely invested in the Napoleonic Wars, which is not something I expected a book about a talking Chinese Celestial dragon to do to me.

[Naomi Novik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Novik) has been quietly building one of the most versatile bibliographies in modern fantasy since that first trilogy landed in 2006. She's moved from alternate military history to fairy-tale-inspired standalones to a magic school that makes Hogwarts look like a health and safety violation waiting to happen, and by 2026 her catalogue sits close to twenty books deep. Here's every series, in the order you should actually read them, plus where a first-timer should start.

## Who Is Naomi Novik?

Novik trained as a computer scientist before she gave it up to write full time, which tracks, because her magic systems are often built like machines: rules that snap together, consequences that compound, no hand-waving. Her breakout idea was simple enough to explain in one sentence, the [Napoleonic Wars](https://www.britannica.com/event/Napoleonic-Wars), but with aerial dragon corps fighting alongside infantry and navy, and complicated enough to sustain nine books without the concept going stale. 

She's since picked up a Hugo, a Nebula, and a small army of devoted readers who will happily fight you over whether Uprooted or Spinning Silver is the better standalone. Her full back catalogue, including short fiction, is listed on [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author/list/8730.Naomi_Novik).

## The Temeraire Series, in Order

This is the one to start with if you came here for dragons and history in equal measure, and it's genuinely one of the best entries on any [best dragon books](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-dragon-books/) list precisely because the dragons aren't decoration. They have politics, culture, and their own chain of command. Read it in publication order, the story is fully serial, no skipping.

### 1. His Majesty's Dragon (2006)

![His Majesty's Dragon by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780345481283-L.jpg)

Captain Will Laurence captures a French ship and finds a dragon egg in the hold. It hatches early, bonds to him on the spot, and his naval career is over before it properly began, dragon riders live and fight apart from "respectable" society. What follows is basically flight school under the threat of French invasion, and it's a fantastic hook for the whole series.

## The Scholomance Trilogy, in Order

Novik's other major series swaps Regency England for a magic school with no teachers, no safety nets, and a body count that would make Hogwarts's staff quit on principle. If someone tells you this is "Harry Potter but dark," they are underselling how genuinely nasty the Scholomance's rules are.

### 1. A Deadly Education (2020)

![A Deadly Education by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780593128503-L.jpg)

El Higgins is a student with terrifying, borderline villain-tier magic and a habit of not using it, because the last time her kind of power got used properly, it ended civilisations. Survival at the Scholomance means making alliances, and El is spectacularly bad at making friends.

Senior year, which is the year the school's monsters get serious about eating the graduating class before they can leave. El's reluctant alliance with golden-boy Orion Lake becomes the emotional spine of the book, and the ending is a genuine gut-punch.

![The Golden Enclaves by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780593158357-L.jpg)

The trilogy closer, dealing with the fallout of graduation and the wider war between the wealthy magical "enclaves" and everyone outside them. It's the most political of the three, and the most satisfying as a conclusion.

## Standalone Fantasy Novels

Novik's standalones are where she experiments outside a fixed universe, and they're also the easiest entry point if nine-book commitments make you nervous.

### Uprooted (2015)

![Uprooted by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780804179058-L.jpg)

A Polish-folklore-inspired fantasy about a village that pays tribute to a wizard called the Dragon (not an actual dragon, sorry) by handing over one girl every ten years. Agnieszka is chosen, expects to be discarded once her decade is up, and instead turns out to be the one with the real power. Dense, wintery, and genuinely inventive with its magic system.

<button data-buy-book data-title="Uprooted" data-author="Naomi Novik" data-isbn="9780804179058">Buy Uprooted</button>

### Spinning Silver (2018)

![Spinning Silver by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780399180989-L.jpg)

Retells Rumpelstiltskin and does it better than the source material has any right to expect from a story that's basically "guy spins straw into gold, demands a baby." Miryem is a moneylender's daughter who can turn silver into gold, metaphorically at first, then very much literally when a fairy king with ice in his veins takes her boast at face value and drags her into his world to prove it. Three narrators, three separate problems, all braided together with the kind of patience Novik's standalones do best. Fewer readers rank it above Uprooted, but the ones who do tend to rank it a lot higher, and I'm one of them.

<button data-buy-book data-title="Spinning Silver" data-author="Naomi Novik" data-isbn="9780399180989">Buy Spinning Silver</button>

### The Summer War (2025)

![The Summer War by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780593984703-L.jpg)

Novik's most recent standalone, and her shortest, a fairy-tale novella about a girl who curses her own brother to a life without love in a fit of childhood fury, then spends the rest of the book trying to undo it. It leans on the same folkloric instincts as Uprooted and Spinning Silver but in a fraction of the page count, and it's a good option if you want to try her fairy-tale mode without the full commitment of a novel.

### Buried Deep and Other Stories (2023)

![Buried Deep and Other Stories by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780593600375-L.jpg)

A short fiction collection, not a novel, but worth mentioning because it collects Novik's scattered novellas and stories in one place, including a Temeraire side-story for anyone who finished League of Dragons and still wanted more. Uneven the way collections tend to be, but the Scholomance-adjacent pieces are worth the cover price alone.

## The One Outlier: Will Supervillains Be on the Final?

### Will Supervillains Be on the Final? (2011)

![Will Supervillains Be on the Final by Naomi Novik](https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780345516565-L.jpg)

This one genuinely surprises people. Between Temeraire books, Novik wrote a shojo-style graphic novel about a superhero college, illustrated by Yishan Li. Leah Taymore is a shy transfer student trying not to get expelled from Liberty Vocational, while a supervillain moonlighting as an ethics professor works his way through the student body. It's a completely different register from anything else on this list, breezy where the rest of her catalogue is dense, and it was clearly meant to launch a longer series that never materialised. Read it as a curiosity rather than a priority, but it's a fun one if you want to see Novik outside her usual mode entirely.

<button data-buy-book data-title="Will Supervillains Be on the Final?" data-author="Naomi Novik" data-isbn="9780345516565">Buy Will Supervillains Be on the Final?</button>

Novik also has one short story tucked inside *Unfettered III: New Tales by Masters of Fantasy* (2019), a multi-author charity anthology alongside the likes of Brandon Sanderson and Robin Hobb. It's not really "a Novik book" in the way anything above is, so it doesn't get its own entry here, but it's worth knowing about if you're a completionist.

## Where Should You Actually Start?

If you want dragons, war, and a slow-burn found-family plot that takes nine books to pay off properly, start with _His Majesty's Dragon_ and commit to the whole run, Temeraire doesn't reward skipping ahead. 

If you want something darker, faster, and finished in three books, the Scholomance is the better entry point, and _A Deadly Education_ is one of the strongest opening chapters in recent fantasy. And if you just want to know what the fuss is about without signing up for a series at all, _Uprooted_ or _Spinning Silver_ will tell you everything you need to know about why people keep putting Novik on their reading lists without needing to track a second or third book down afterwards.

Whichever door you go through, log it before you forget half the dragon names like I did somewhere around book six.
