Crime thrillers start with a body, a case or a plan, and most of the pleasure is in watching someone work it through. It might be a detective closing in on a killer, a private eye following a lie, or a man in the wrong place with a bag of money.

The twelve books below were published between 1939 and 2007, and they cover most of what the genre does: cops in Boston and Los Angeles, a Moscow investigator, a Swedish inspector, private detectives, a drifter, an FBI profiler and a Texas sheriff. Some are fast. Some are bleak and slow.

## What makes a good crime thriller

Crime fiction is a huge shelf, and plenty of it is forgettable. The books that stay with you tend to have a few things in common:

- **A detective with a life.** Someone tired, stubborn or compromised enough to feel like a person, not a method.
- **A place that matters.** Boston, Los Angeles, Moscow and southern Sweden are all characters here.
- **A case that costs something.** The best ones leave the investigator worse off, and sometimes leave the reader worse off too.
- **A solution that earns its place.** It doesn't have to be a twist, but it has to feel like the only way the story could end.

## The 12 best crime thriller books

### 1. The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo by Stieg Larsson

![Cover of The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81YW99XIpJL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.18 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2429135.The_Girl_With_the_Dragon_Tattoo) from more than 3.5 million ratings. First published 2005.*

Mikael Blomkvist, a journalist who has just lost a libel case, is hired by an elderly industrialist to find out what happened to his great-niece Harriet, who disappeared in 1966. Lisbeth Salander, a young hacker with a violent history and her own rules, joins the investigation, and together they dig into the secrets of the Vanger family.

The Swedish title was *Män som hatar kvinnor*, "Men Who Hate Women", and Larsson died before the book was published. It's long and takes a while to get going, but Salander is one of the great characters of modern crime fiction. It is the first of the Millennium series. Heads-up: it includes graphic sexual violence.

### 2. In the Woods by Tana French

![Cover of In the Woods](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/91jMHH2+qHL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[3.78 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2459785.In_the_Woods) from about 454,000 ratings. First published 2007.*

Detective Rob Ryan is assigned the murder of a twelve-year-old girl, found at an archaeological dig in Knocknaree, a Dublin suburb. In 1984, in the same woods, Ryan was found as a boy while his two friends vanished, and he remembers nothing.

It is the first Dublin Murder Squad novel and won the Edgar Award for Best First Novel. French leaves the 1984 mystery unsolved on purpose, and some readers never forgive her for it. If you need every thread tied off, skip it. If you can live with that, the writing is excellent, and Ryan is an unreliable, self-deceiving narrator you stay with anyway.

### 3. Mystic River by Dennis Lehane

![Cover of Mystic River](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81u38NXpkrL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.18 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/21671.Mystic_River) from about 174,000 ratings. First published 2001.*

Three boys grow up in the same Boston neighbourhood. One of them, Dave Boyle, is abducted off the street at eleven and escapes days later, and none of the three is the same afterwards. Twenty-five years on, Jimmy Marcus's nineteen-year-old daughter is murdered. Sean Devine is the homicide detective on the case, and Dave came home that night covered in someone else's blood.

The case matters less than the guilt underneath it. The ending is devastating because it feels so inevitable. The book won the Anthony Award and the Barry Award for Best Novel, and Clint Eastwood's 2003 film earned Oscars for Sean Penn and Tim Robbins. Lehane has two books on this page, and this is the better place to begin.

### 4. The Black Echo by Michael Connelly

![Cover of The Black Echo](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71bhkjFizPL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.15 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/32508.The_Black_Echo) from about 222,000 ratings. First published 1992.*

Harry Bosch is an LAPD homicide detective in Hollywood Division, and a Vietnam veteran who spent the war crawling through Viet Cong tunnels. When a body turns up in a drainpipe off Mulholland Dam, it is a man Bosch served with there, and the case leads back to a bank robbery carried out through an underground tunnel.

Connelly was a crime reporter at the *Los Angeles Times* before he wrote this, and it shows in the procedure. *The Black Echo* won the Edgar Award for Best First Novel and began a series that has run for three decades, with a long-running TV adaptation. It is a good first Bosch case and a good place to find out whether you want the rest.

### 5. Killing Floor by Lee Child

![Cover of Killing Floor](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81+jmuAM87L._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.08 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/78129.Killing_Floor) from about 362,000 ratings. First published 1997.*

Jack Reacher, a former military policeman now drifting around America, gets off a bus in Margrave, Georgia. Within hours he is arrested for a murder he did not commit. As he works out who did, he finds the town is hiding something much bigger.

Child began writing after losing his television job in 1995, and *Killing Floor* was his first novel. Reacher is blunt and nearly indestructible, and the plot moves at the pace of an action film. It is the first of a series that has run to more than twenty-five novels and has been adapted for television. If you want a straightforward, fast crime thriller, this is the easiest book on the list.

### 6. L.A. Confidential by James Ellroy

![Cover of L.A. Confidential](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81KWDCha2KL._SL1269_.jpg)

*[4.19 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1134753) from about 41,000 ratings. First published 1990.*

Los Angeles, the early 1950s. A massacre at an all-night coffee shop pulls three very different LAPD cops into the same case: Ed Exley, a rule-bound careerist, Bud White, a brute with a soft spot for women who are being hurt, and Jack Vincennes, a narcotics detective who consults on a TV cop show. As they investigate, the case opens onto corruption that reaches well beyond the shooting.

Ellroy writes in short, clipped, slang-heavy sentences, and the plot is dense, so expect to concentrate for the first hundred pages. It is the third book in his L.A. Quartet, but it works as its own novel. The 1997 film adaptation is excellent and much simpler than the book. Heads-up: the violence and the period racism are both strong.

### 7. Red Dragon by Thomas Harris

![Cover of Red Dragon](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81dkf1dFePL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.07 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/28877.Red_Dragon) from about 386,000 ratings. First published 1981.*

Will Graham is a former FBI profiler who left after catching Hannibal Lecter and nearly dying doing it. When a killer known as the Tooth Fairy starts murdering whole families under the full moon, his old boss persuades him back. Graham's method is to think like the killer, and that is the problem, because it costs him more each time.

This is where Lecter first appeared, and Graham ends up consulting him from his cell. Lecter is a smaller part of this book than you'd expect, and the real subject is the killer, Francis Dolarhyde, who is written with surprising sympathy. It was filmed as *Manhunter* in 1986 and again as *Red Dragon* in 2002, and it is the first Lecter book, so it's the right place to start the series.

### 8. No Country for Old Men by Cormac McCarthy

![Cover of No Country for Old Men](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81qyisdd86L._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.17 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/538837.No_Country_for_Old_Men) from about 271,000 ratings. First published 2005.*

Llewelyn Moss is hunting antelope in the Texas desert when he comes across the wreckage of a drug deal gone wrong: bodies, a truck full of heroin and a case holding two million dollars. He takes the money. Anton Chigurh, a killer who treats murder as a matter of principle, is sent to get it back, and Sheriff Ed Tom Bell follows the trail of bodies a step behind both of them.

McCarthy wrote it first as a screenplay, and it reads fast, with bare dialogue and no quotation marks. Between the chapters, Bell reflects on a county that has changed in ways he doesn't understand, and that is what gives the book its weight. The Coen brothers' 2007 film won the Oscar for Best Picture. Heads-up: it is very violent.

### 9. Gorky Park by Martin Cruz Smith

![Cover of Gorky Park](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71tfpQcIToS._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.03 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13240) from about 71,000 ratings. First published 1981.*

Three bodies are found frozen in the snow of Gorky Park in Moscow, their faces and fingertips cut off to make identification impossible. The chief homicide investigator, Arkady Renko, is a decent man in an indecent system, and the closer he gets to the truth the more it looks like a case the KGB would prefer him to drop.

Renko is a quiet, sceptical hero, and the book is as much about how to be honest in a country that doesn't reward it as it is about the murders. An American detective and a New York fur trader, both with their own reasons for being in Moscow, make the investigation harder. It is the first of a long Renko series, and it was filmed in 1983 with William Hurt.

### 10. Gone, Baby, Gone by Dennis Lehane

![Cover of Gone, Baby, Gone](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/817oI1at-mL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.16 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/425123.Gone_Baby_Gone) from about 44,000 ratings. First published 1998.*

Patrick Kenzie and Angie Gennaro are private investigators in Dorchester, Boston. When a four-year-old girl, Amanda McCready, disappears, her aunt hires them to supplement the police search, and it soon becomes clear that her mother is not telling them everything. The deeper they look, the less anyone on either side of the law seems to want the truth.

It is the fourth book in the Kenzie and Gennaro series, and the case stands alone, though the earlier books give the partnership more weight. The book's real subject is a moral choice at the end, and readers still argue about it, which is why it is on this list rather than just another Lehane. Ben Affleck directed the 2007 film. Heads-up: it deals with child abuse and neglect.

### 11. Faceless Killers by Henning Mankell

![Cover of Faceless Killers](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71j4GXq4kxL._SL1170_.jpg)

*[3.76 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/935308.Faceless_Killers) from about 81,000 ratings. First published 1991.*

In a remote farmhouse in southern Sweden, an elderly farmer is beaten to death and his wife left for dead. Before she dies, she says one word: "foreign". Inspector Kurt Wallander, of the Ystad police, is overweight, worn down and recently left by his wife, and he has to investigate a murder as the press and the public start looking for someone to blame.

The Swedish title was *Mördare utan ansikte*. Mankell uses a police procedural to look at what his country has become, and Wallander, a decent man who is tired of the job, is the opposite of the usual brilliant detective. The Wallander series ran for nearly two decades and is often cited as an ancestor of Nordic noir. It has the lowest rating on this list, and the pace is slower than the others, but it is a very good book.

### 12. The Big Sleep by Raymond Chandler

![Cover of The Big Sleep](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71m7lCNOpWL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[3.94 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2052.The_Big_Sleep) from about 174,000 ratings. First published 1939.*

Philip Marlowe is a private investigator in Los Angeles who is hired by a dying millionaire, General Sternwood, to deal with a blackmailer. Sternwood has two wild daughters, one of whom has a talent for getting into trouble, and Marlowe soon has a murder, a disappearance and several people who aren't being honest with him.

It was Chandler's first novel, and the plot is famously tangled. The story goes that when the film's makers asked who had killed the chauffeur, Chandler replied that he didn't know. What carries the book is the voice: Marlowe's wisecracks and similes, and the sense that he's an honest man in a city that isn't. It is the first of seven Marlowe novels, and it set the standard for the lone private eye.

## Where to start if you're new to crime thrillers

If you want clear momentum, start with *Killing Floor* or *The Black Echo*. Both are the first of long series, so there is plenty more if you like them.

If you want something with more weight, try *Mystic River* or *No Country for Old Men*. They are both violent, both very well written, and both have endings that you won't forget.

If you like atmosphere and a sense of place, *Gorky Park* and *Faceless Killers* are the ones to try. For voice, go straight to *The Big Sleep*, and for something dense and demanding, *L.A. Confidential*.

## Crime thriller books FAQ

**What is the best crime thriller book to start with?**
*Killing Floor* by Lee Child is the easiest way in, with a 4.08 Goodreads rating from about 362,000 ratings. If you want something more serious, start with *Mystic River*.

**What is the difference between a crime thriller and a mystery?**
A mystery is usually a puzzle about who did it. A crime thriller is more about the pressure: the investigator is in danger, there's a clock running, or the reader knows more than the characters do. Many books do both.

**Which book on this list has the highest Goodreads rating?**
*L.A. Confidential* has the highest at 4.19. *The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo* and *Mystic River* follow at 4.18, and *No Country for Old Men* at 4.17.

**Which of these books are standalone novels?**
*Mystic River* and *No Country for Old Men* are standalone novels. *L.A. Confidential* is the third book in Ellroy's L.A. Quartet but works on its own, and *Gone, Baby, Gone* is the fourth Kenzie and Gennaro book, though the case stands alone. The rest are the first book in a series: the Millennium books, Dublin Murder Squad, Harry Bosch, Jack Reacher, Hannibal Lecter, Arkady Renko, Kurt Wallander and Philip Marlowe.

**Are any of these books very violent?**
Several are. *The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo*, *L.A. Confidential* and *No Country for Old Men* are the most graphic, and *Gone, Baby, Gone* deals with child neglect. I've put a heads-up on each of the books above.
