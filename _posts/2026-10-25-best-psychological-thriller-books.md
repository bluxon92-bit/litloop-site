In a psychological thriller, the danger is mostly in people's heads. The question is who is lying, who is remembering it wrong, and who you've been trusting for too long.

The twelve books below are all about that. Some have narrators who can't be believed, and a few tell you what happened in the opening pages and make you wait to find out why. They were published between 1950 and 2019, and they range from fast weekend reads to long, slow novels that sit with you for weeks.

Several of them deal with heavy subjects, including self-harm, abuse, school violence and harm to children. I've put a heads-up on each book where it applies.

If you want the wider classics, see our list of the [best thrillers of all time](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-thrillers-of-all-time/), and for detectives and cases, our [best crime thriller books](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-crime-thriller-books/). No book appears in more than one of the three.

**Short on time?** Read *The Silent Patient* for a quick, clean twist, *Misery* for pure dread in one room, or *The Secret History* for a long, slow read.

## What makes a good psychological thriller

You don't need a body count. What you need is a reason to doubt what you're being told. The best psychological thrillers tend to share these:

- **A narrator or character you can't fully trust.** Memory, drink, obsession or self-interest all work.
- **Information held back, not hidden.** A good one plays fair, so that when the truth comes out you can see where it was.
- **An ordinary setting.** A marriage, a school, a commuter train. The scarier the book, the more ordinary it looks.
- **A twist that changes meaning, not just facts.** If it only swaps one culprit for another, it won't survive a second read.

## The 12 best psychological thriller books

### 1. Sharp Objects by Gillian Flynn

![Cover of Sharp Objects](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/711GMokDpAL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.00 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18045891-sharp-objects) from more than 1.2 million ratings. First published 2006.*

Camille Preaker is a newspaper reporter with a drinking habit and a history she would rather forget. Her editor sends her back to Wind Gap, Missouri, to cover the murder of one girl and the disappearance of another. The town's main industry is hog butchering, her family owns the biggest operation, and the family house turns out to be where the real story is.

It was Flynn's debut, and it won both the CWA New Blood Dagger and the Ian Fleming Steel Dagger in 2007. It is nastier and sadder than *Gone Girl*, and the ending is bleak and doesn't try to comfort you. It was adapted by HBO in 2018 with Amy Adams. Heads-up: it deals with self-harm and violence against children.

### 2. The Silent Patient by Alex Michaelides

![Cover of The Silent Patient](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81y9uCHoxrL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.16 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40097951-the-silent-patient) from more than 3.6 million ratings. First published 2019.*

Alicia Berenson, a painter, shoots her husband five times in the face and never speaks again. Six years later, psychotherapist Theo Faber takes a job at the secure unit in North London where she is held, certain that he can get her to talk.

It was Michaelides's debut novel, and it won the 2019 Goodreads Choice Award for Mystery & Thriller. It is short and quick, and it builds to a twist that sends you back to the first chapter to see how you missed it. Readers are split on whether the twist is earned, which makes it a good one to argue about.

### 3. Before I Go to Sleep by S.J. Watson

![Cover of Before I Go to Sleep](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81gXotZKEhL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[3.90 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9736930-before-i-go-to-sleep) from about 375,000 ratings. First published 2011.*

Christine Lucas wakes up every morning with no memory of her adult life, in bed next to a man who says he is her husband. On the advice of a doctor, she starts keeping a secret journal, writing down what she has learned each night and reading it back each morning.

The dread comes from the entries and the people around her slowly failing to line up. Watson's debut won the CWA John Creasey Dagger and has sold millions of copies around the world, so it is hardly a secret, but it gets mentioned less than some of the others here. It was filmed in 2014 with Nicole Kidman and Colin Firth.

### 4. The Girl on the Train by Paula Hawkins

![Cover of The Girl on the Train](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81O-JjbDfML._SL1500_.jpg)

*[3.96 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/22557272-the-girl-on-the-train) from more than 3.3 million ratings. First published 2015.*

Rachel Watson takes the same commuter train every morning, and every morning it stops at the same signal, beside the same row of back gardens. She has invented a perfect life for the couple she sees in one of them. Then she sees something she shouldn't, the woman goes missing, and Rachel, who drinks, who has blackouts and who has a history with the street, is both a witness and a suspect.

The story is told by three women, Rachel, Megan and Anna, and none of them is reliable. It won the 2015 Goodreads Choice Award for Mystery & Thriller and was filmed in 2016 with Emily Blunt. It is a fast read.

### 5. Verity by Colleen Hoover

![Cover of Verity](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81HVQp-OMfL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.28 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/41957126.Verity) from more than 4.2 million ratings. First published 2018.*

Lowen Ashleigh is a struggling writer who gets a big offer: to finish the bestselling series of Verity Crawford, a novelist who can no longer write after an accident. Lowen moves into the Crawford house to go through Verity's notes, and in the study she finds a hidden manuscript, an autobiography in which Verity confesses to things no one should ever read.

The book is built around that manuscript, and the question of whether to believe it. It is the highest-rated book on this list apart from *Big Little Lies*, and it is one of the books that took off on BookTok. Heads-up: it has explicit sex scenes and contains disturbing material involving children.

### 6. Shutter Island by Dennis Lehane

![Cover of Shutter Island](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81fp8q4d3ZL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.14 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/21686.Shutter_Island) from about 241,000 ratings. First published 2003.*

It is 1954. U.S. Marshal Teddy Daniels and his new partner, Chuck Aule, travel by ferry to Ashecliffe Hospital, a facility for the criminally insane on an island in Boston Harbor. A patient, Rachel Solando, has vanished from a locked room, leaving a note that makes no sense. A hurricane is coming, and the staff are not telling them everything.

Lehane builds the atmosphere well, and Daniels has demons of his own that surface as the storm comes in. Martin Scorsese filmed it in 2010 with Leonardo DiCaprio, and I'd still read the book if you've seen the film. Lehane also has two books on our [crime thriller list](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-crime-thriller-books/).

### 7. Misery by Stephen King

![Cover of Misery](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81J7KCM51ZL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.22 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/10614.Misery) from about 864,000 ratings. First published 1987.*

Paul Sheldon is a bestselling novelist who crashes his car in a Colorado blizzard and wakes up in the home of Annie Wilkes, a former nurse who says she is his number one fan. She has read his latest book, in which he killed off his heroine, Misery Chastain, and she is not pleased. She tells him he will write a new one, and she will not let him leave until he does.

Almost all of it takes place in one house, with two characters, and it is one of the tightest things King has written. It is as much about a writer's relationship with his readers as it is about horror. The 1990 film earned Kathy Bates the Oscar for Best Actress. Heads-up: there are scenes of graphic violence.

### 8. The Secret History by Donna Tartt

![Cover of The Secret History](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81YhQfeiynL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.14 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/29044.The_Secret_History) from more than 1.1 million ratings. First published 1992.*

Richard Papen leaves California for Hampden College, a small liberal arts school in Vermont, and talks his way into a small, close group of students who study Ancient Greek with a charismatic professor, Julian Morrow. The book opens by telling you that the group has killed one of its own, and that Richard was part of it. The rest is the story of how it came to that, and what happened afterwards.

It's a howdunit and a whydunit, not a whodunit, and Tartt spends her time on the people: their snobbery, their loyalty and their growing fear. It is long and slow, and the first few chapters take patience. Tartt later won the Pulitzer Prize for *The Goldfinch*.

### 9. Strangers on a Train by Patricia Highsmith

![Cover of Strangers on a Train](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/413xSVokSUL.jpg)

*[3.72 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/25622296-strangers-on-a-train) from about 41,000 ratings. First published 1950.*

Guy Haines, a young architect, meets a stranger called Charles Bruno on a train. Bruno has an idea: he will kill Guy's unfaithful wife, and Guy will kill Bruno's father, and with no connection between the victims and the killers, neither will be caught. Guy thinks it's a joke. Bruno does not.

It was Highsmith's first novel, and it is a study of how easily someone can be pulled into a plan they said no to. Hitchcock filmed it in 1951 and changed the ending, so the book is darker. If you like the author, her best-known novel, *The Talented Mr Ripley*, is on our [best thrillers of all time](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-thrillers-of-all-time/) list.

### 10. We Need to Talk About Kevin by Lionel Shriver

![Cover of We Need to Talk About Kevin](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/91AuFmU8jtL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.08 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/80660.We_Need_to_Talk_About_Kevin) from about 223,000 ratings. First published 2003.*

Eva Khatchadourian writes letters to her husband, Franklin, about their son Kevin, who committed a school massacre two days before his sixteenth birthday. Over the course of the book she goes back through Kevin's childhood, and her own ambivalence about being a mother, trying to work out what she saw and what she missed.

The question that runs through it is whether Eva is a reliable witness to her own life. It won the Orange Prize in 2005, which is now the Women's Prize for Fiction, and was filmed in 2011 with Tilda Swinton. It is the heaviest book on the list and not an easy read. Heads-up: it deals with a school massacre and with violence, including towards children.

### 11. Big Little Lies by Liane Moriarty

![Cover of Big Little Lies](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71ghdjL2A3L._SL1500_.jpg)

*[4.31 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33516773-big-little-lies) from about 1.16 million ratings. First published 2014.*

A school trivia night in an Australian beach town ends in a death. The book then goes back six months to show how three mothers, Madeline, Celeste and Jane, got there, with the gossip of the other parents running through it like a chorus. Each of the three is hiding something, and a playground incident between five-year-olds is where it begins.

It is the lightest book on this list in tone, with a lot of dry humour, and the darkest in what it eventually shows. It has the highest rating here, and it was adapted by HBO in 2017 with Reese Witherspoon and Nicole Kidman. Heads-up: one storyline deals with domestic abuse.

### 12. Notes on a Scandal by Zoë Heller

![Cover of Notes on a Scandal](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/81z9yt-TxuL._SL1500_.jpg)

*[3.74 on Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/17018.Notes_on_a_Scandal) from about 26,000 ratings. First published 2003.*

Barbara Covett is an older history teacher at a London comprehensive, lonely and exacting, who decides that the new art teacher, Sheba Hart, will be her friend. When Barbara finds out that Sheba is having an affair with a fifteen-year-old pupil, she sees her chance, and she writes up the whole story herself.

That is the trick of the book: Barbara tells it, and she is not on anyone's side but her own. It was shortlisted for the Booker Prize in 2003, and in the US it was published as *What Was She Thinking?* A 2006 film starred Judi Dench and Cate Blanchett. It is short and sharp, and a good one for a book club. Heads-up: the affair involves a minor.

## Where to start if you're new to psychological thrillers

If you want something quick that you can finish in a weekend, start with *The Silent Patient* or *The Girl on the Train*. If you want memory and doubt, *Before I Go to Sleep*.

If you want something darker, *Sharp Objects* is the next step. *Misery* is the pick for pure dread in one room, and *Shutter Island* if you like a storm and a locked-down building.

If you want something slower and more literary, try *The Secret History*, *Notes on a Scandal* or *Strangers on a Train*. For something light on the surface and rotten underneath, try *Big Little Lies*. ## Psychological thriller books FAQ

**What is a psychological thriller?**
A psychological thriller gets its tension from what is going on inside a character's mind or between people: an unreliable narrator, lost memory, a marriage you can't read. A crime thriller is built around a crime and the investigation. Many books do both, and we cover the other side in our [best crime thriller books](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-crime-thriller-books/).

**What is the best psychological thriller to start with?**
*The Silent Patient* is the simplest way in. It is quick, self-contained, and has a 4.16 rating from more than 3.6 million Goodreads readers. *Gone Girl* is the other usual starting point, and it's in our [best thrillers of all time](https://www.litloop.co/blog/best-thrillers-of-all-time/) list.

**Which book on this list has the highest Goodreads rating?**
*Big Little Lies* at 4.31, followed by *Verity* at 4.28 and *Misery* at 4.22.

**Are any of these books standalone?**
Yes. All twelve can be read on their own, and none needs anything else read first.

**Which of these books should I avoid if I'm sensitive to certain subjects?**
Check the heads-up on each entry. In short: *Sharp Objects* (self-harm, violence against children), *Verity* (explicit sex and harm to children), *We Need to Talk About Kevin* (school shooting), *Big Little Lies* (domestic abuse) and *Notes on a Scandal* (a relationship with a minor). *Misery* is graphically violent.
